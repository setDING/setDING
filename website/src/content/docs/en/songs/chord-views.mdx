setDING shows the chords of a song in three ways:

- **Chords** – as names, the way they are written in the song, e.g. `Am7`.
- **Nashville** – as Nashville numbers, e.g. `6m7`.
- **Numerals** – as Roman numerals, as in harmonic analysis, e.g. `vi7`.

Numbers and numerals show **where a chord sits in the key**, not which note it is.
When the key changes, they stay the same: if you think `ii – V – I`, you can play
the song in any key. The view only changes what you see; the song itself stays as
it is, also for the rest of the band.

## Where to choose the view

- In the [settings](/en/settings/display/) under **Show chords as**, for all songs.
- In the [personal adjustments](/en/songs/personal/) of a song under **Chord view**,
  for this song only.
- In [live mode](/en/live/play/) in the control bar with **C** (chords), **1**
  (Nashville) and **I** (numerals), also for this song only.

<Image src={menu} alt="The personal adjustments of a song with the chord view menu open: Chords, Nashville and Numerals" />

Numbers and numerals need a **key in the song** (`{key: C}`). Without one, setDING
shows the chord names and tells you why.

## How to read numerals

### The number is the scale degree

Every note of the major scale has a degree from **I** to **VII**. In C:

| Note | C | D | E | F | G | A | B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Degree | I | II | III | IV | V | VI | VII |

A chord gets the degree of its root. In G, `D` is `V`; in F, it is `VI`.

### Upper or lower case shows the quality

- **Upper case** are major chords, including dominant sevenths like `G7` and
  augmented chords: `I`, `IV`, `V7`, `I+`.
- **Lower case** are minor chords and diminished and half-diminished ones: `ii`,
  `vi7`, `vii°`, `viiø7`.

So there is no `m` for minor: `Am7` in C is `vi7`, not `vim7`. If you would
rather read `VIm7`, choose the other [spelling](#two-spellings).

### Symbols and extensions

| Chord in C | Numeral | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `C` | `I` | major |
| `Dm` | `ii` | minor |
| `G7` | `V7` | dominant seventh |
| `Cmaj7` | `IΔ7` | major with a major seventh (`Δ`) |
| `Am(maj7)` | `viΔ7` | minor with a major seventh |
| `Bdim` / `Bdim7` | `vii°` / `vii°7` | diminished (`°`) |
| `Bm7b5` | `viiø7` | half-diminished (`ø`) |
| `Caug` | `I+` | augmented (`+`) |
| `Csus4` | `Isus4` | fourth instead of the third |
| `G7b9` | `V7♭9` | extensions and alterations as in the chord |

In the spelling **Case**, setDING always writes numerals this way, whatever the
[chord style](/en/settings/display/). With **Raise extensions**, extensions and
alterations are smaller and raised, as with chords.

### Accidentals before the number

When a chord is not on a degree of the major scale, a `♭` or `♯` stands **before**
the number: in C, `Bb` is `♭VII`, `Eb` is `♭III`, `Ab` is `♭VI` and `Db` is `♭II`.

### Bass notes

For a chord with a bass note, the bass is an **arabic number** after the slash, as
with Nashville: `C/E` is `I/3`, `G/B` is `V/7`, `C/Bb` is `I/♭7`.

## Two spellings

Roman numerals are written in two common ways. Both are correct; which one you
read more easily depends on where you learned them:

| Spelling | Minor chord | ii–V–I in C | Common in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Case** | `vi` | `ii7 – V7 – IΔ7` | classical harmony, much of pop and jazz teaching |
| **Chord symbol** | `VIm` | `IIm7 – V7 – Imaj7` | jazz charts and Berklee-style analysis |

With **Case** (the default), upper and lower case show the quality, as explained
above. With **Chord symbol**, the numeral is always upper case and the chord's
ending follows it, spelled in your [chord style](/en/settings/display/), just like
the Nashville numbers: `IIm7` with **Standard**, `II−7` with **Jazz**, and as
written in the song with **As written**. `Bm7b5` in C is then `VIIm7b5` or `VIIø7`.

Choose the spelling in the [settings](/en/settings/display/) under **Numeral
spelling**, right below **Show chords as**. It applies to all songs.

Everything else on this page applies to both spellings: accidentals, bass notes
and minor keys (`Im – ♭III – ♭VII` with chord symbols).

## Minor keys

In minor, setDING counts the degrees as in jazz, **from the major scale**, with
accidentals for the lowered degrees. In A minor:

| Chord in Am | `Am` | `Bm7b5` | `C` | `Dm7` | `E7` | `F` | `G` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Numeral | `i` | `iiø7` | `♭III` | `iv7` | `V7` | `♭VI` | `♭VII` |

Classical harmony often counts from the minor scale and writes `III`, `VI` and
`VII` without accidentals. setDING keeps the jazz way, so numerals and Nashville
numbers agree (`b3`, `b6`, `b7`).

## What setDING does not analyse

- **Each chord on its own:** setDING writes the degree of every chord without
  interpreting the harmony. `D7` in C is `II7`, not `V/V` (secondary dominant).
- **No inversion figures:** inversions are written with the bass note (`I/3`), not
  as figured bass like `I⁶`.
- **Unknown chords** get an upper-case numeral and their ending as written in the
  song, so no chord is shown wrongly or left out.
