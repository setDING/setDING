Songs are written in [ChordPro](https://www.chordpro.org/), the open format for
chord sheets: chords go in square brackets right inside the lyrics, details such as
the title or key in curly brackets. [ChordPro syntax](/en/songs/chordpro/) explains
the notation and the setDING directives for the song sequence and drums.

```
{title: Amazing Grace}
{key: G}

{start_of_verse}
A[G]mazing [G7]grace, how [C]sweet the [G]sound
{end_of_verse}
```

Open the editor with **New song**, or in a song with the pencil (**Edit ChordPro**)
at the start of the toolbar. If the song has ChordPro problems, the pencil carries a
warning badge and the editor jumps straight to the first place.

## Text and preview

On wide screens the text and the preview are side by side; on narrow ones you switch
between **Text** and **Preview**. The preview shows the song the way the band sees
it.

## Insert instead of typing

The bar above the text inserts what you need without you having to know the
ChordPro syntax:

- **Insert chord**, **Insert verse**, **Insert chorus** and **Insert comment** right
  in the bar.
- **Chords** – e.g. **Optional chord**, **No chord (N.C.)**, **Chord diagram**.
- **Sections** – intro, bridge, solo, outro and more, **Tab**, **Chord grid** and
  **Repeat chorus**.
- **Song info** – **Title**, **Artist**, **Key**, **Capo**, **Tempo (BPM)**, **Time
  signature**, **Song sequence**, **CCLI number** and more.
- **Text** – comments, **Bold**, **Italic**, **Colour** and more.
- **More** – **Column break**, **Page break**, **Image**, **ABC notation** and more.

With lines selected, a section (e.g. **Insert chorus**) puts its start and end around
these lines, even when the selection starts or ends in the middle of a line.

**Song details** edits the title, key, tempo and other details in a form. While you
type, the editor suggests matching chords and directives. Chords are suggested in
alphabetical order, after typing `[` and after **Insert chord**.

After `{chorus: ` the editor suggests the labels of the song's choruses, so a repeat
has the same name as its chorus. `{chorus}` always repeats the last chorus before it;
the label only changes the name shown.

<Image src={editorChorusLabels} alt="The editor with the suggestions Chorus and Final chorus below a line {chorus: }" />

## Closing brackets and sections

The editor closes what you open:

- When you type `[` or `{`, it puts the closing bracket right after the cursor. If
  you type it yourself anyway, the cursor just steps over it. **Backspace** right
  after an empty pair `[]` deletes both.
- With text selected, `[` or `{` puts the brackets around the selection.
- In a directive, `"` after `=` or `:` is closed too, e.g. in
  `{start_of_chorus label="…"}`. Here too the cursor steps over the closing
  `"`, and **Backspace** deletes an empty `""` completely.
- **Enter** at the end of a section start such as `{start_of_chorus}` adds an empty
  line and the matching end `{end_of_chorus}`, with the cursor on the empty line.
  Choosing `start_of_…` from the suggestions does the same. If the section is
  already closed, Enter just starts a new line. With the start and the end on the
  same line, e.g. `{start_of_verse}{end_of_verse}`, **Enter** between them puts them
  on lines of their own with an empty line between.
- When you change the name of a section start, e.g. `{start_of_verse}` to
  `{start_of_chorus}`, the editor changes its end `{end_of_verse}` along with it, and
  the other way round. Short forms stay short (`{sov}`/`{eov}` become `{soc}`/`{eoc}`).

In comment lines (`#`), after `\` and in tab and notation sections (ABC, LilyPond),
brackets stay as you type them. If you prefer to type everything yourself, turn the
aids off in the settings under [General](/en/settings/general/) with **Close brackets
and sections automatically**.

## Edit lines

**Lines** has **Move line up**, **Move line down**, **Duplicate line**, **Delete
line** and **Comment out line (#)**, plus **Move chord left** and **Move chord
right** to move a chord by one syllable. Tapping a line number selects the whole
line. **Undo** and **Redo** step back and forth.

## Search and replace

**Search** finds text in the song, with **Match case** if you like. **Replace**
replaces single matches, **Replace all** all of them.

## Chords above lyrics

Many chord sheets on the internet write the chords on a line of their own above the
lyrics. When you paste such a text, setDING offers to convert it (**Convert**). For
text that is already in the editor: **Convert chords above lyrics**.

## Bar lines as chord grids

Some songs, e.g. from CCLI SongSelect, write instrumental parts as bar lines in
chord brackets, e.g. `[|][G/B] [C2] [.] [.][|]`. The editor marks such lines with a
dotted line and shows a hint above the text: **Convert** turns them into a chord
grid, with the comment above as its label. Measures with fewer chords are filled
up with empty cells so the columns line up. For single blocks: select them and
use **Convert bar lines to chord grids** in the Lines menu. The hint can be
hidden with ✕.

<Image src={editorCcli} alt="The editor with two blocks of CCLI bar lines marked with a dotted line and the hint with the Convert button" />

## CCLI directives

Songs from CCLI SongSelect contain `{ccli: …}` and `{ccli_license: …}`, which the
ChordPro specification does not know. The import converts them; for songs saved
earlier, the editor shows a hint above the text: **Convert** renames `{ccli}` to
`{x_ccli}` and removes `{ccli_license}`, the licence number of the church that
downloaded the song. The hint can be hidden with ✕.

<Image src={editorSongSelect} alt="The editor with the hint about two CCLI directives that do not follow the ChordPro specification and the Convert button" />

## CCLI footer

ChordPro files from CCLI SongSelect end with a paragraph with the CCLI song number,
the copyright, the terms of use and the CCLI licence number. The import removes it.
For songs that still contain it, the editor shows a hint above the text:
**Remove** deletes the paragraph and keeps song number and copyright in the song
details (`{x_ccli}`, `{copyright}`), unless the song already has them. The hint can be hidden with ✕.

<Image src={editorCcliFooter} alt="The editor with the CCLI footer at the end of the song and the hint with the Remove button" />

## Section labels

The ChordPro specification recommends writing a section's label as an attribute:
`{start_of_verse: label="Verse 1"}`. Songs from other sources often use the short
form `{start_of_verse: Verse 1}`. setDING shows both the same, but the editor shows
a hint above the text: **Convert** writes all labels in the recommended form. The
hint can be hidden with ✕.

<Image src={editorLabels} alt="The editor with the hint about section labels in the short form and the Convert button; the second verse already uses the label attribute" />

## Save

**Save** (✓, top right) saves the song for everyone. The button turns from a pale to
a filled colour as soon as you change something, so you see at a glance that there
are unsaved changes. On narrow screens it shows only the ✓.

<Image src={editorSave} alt="The editor with unsaved changes: the Save button at the top right is filled" />

If setDING finds places while saving that will probably not
be shown as intended – e.g. a bracket that is never closed – it asks **Check the
ChordPro?** and names the lines. You can **Fix automatically**, **Keep editing** or
**Save anyway**.

**Esc** closes the editor like the arrow at the top left. If you changed something,
setDING first asks whether to discard the changes. With the search or a list of
suggestions open, Esc closes that first.

To see everything ChordPro can do, import the sample song
[`feature-showcase.zip`](https://github.com/tafli/setDING/raw/main/docs/examples/feature-showcase.zip)
and open it in the editor.
