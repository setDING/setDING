A song is stored the way it was written. How your band plays it – in which key, in
which sequence, at which tempo – you set without changing the song. setDING has two
levels for this:

1. **Band arrangement:** how the band always plays the song. Applies in all the
   band's sets.
2. **Set:** what is different tonight, e.g. two semitones lower or without the
   second verse. Applies in this set only.

What is set in the set wins; otherwise the band arrangement applies, otherwise the
song. The band's **Leader** and **Set planner** and the admins of the organization
may change both. The other bands of the organization are not affected, and every
musician can still transpose or set a capo just for themselves on top.

## The band arrangement

1. On **Sets**, select the band.
2. Open the song on **Songs** and tap **Band arrangement** at the top.
3. Set:
   - the key with **Key of the band – for all its sets**,
   - the sequence with **Sequence**,
   - the tempo with **Tempo** (empty means: as in the song).

If you play in several bands of the organization, you can also choose the band at the
top of the band arrangement. It is the same choice as on **Sets**: the song list, the
sets and live mode follow it.

**Reset the band arrangement to the song** removes the arrangement again. In the
song list, a band icon next to a song shows that the band has an arrangement for it.

## The key in the set

Each song of the set shows its key on the right. Tap it and pick one of the twelve
keys (**Key in this set – for the whole band**). If the band has an arrangement, its
key is the starting point: "−2" means two semitones below the band's key.

## The song sequence in the set

The song sequence says in which order the parts of the song are played, e.g.
`I, V1, C, V2, C x2, B, O` (intro, verse 1, chorus, verse 2, chorus twice, bridge,
outro).

1. In the song's ⋮ menu, tap **Song sequence for this set**.
2. Under **Parts of the song**, tap the parts in the order you play them, or drag
   them into the sequence. Long-press a part to move it; **Clear** starts over.
3. **Edit as text** lets you type the sequence directly.

<Image src={sequence} alt="Building the song sequence for a set from the parts of the song" />

**Use the song's sequence** removes a set sequence again. In live mode, an icon next
to the sequence shows where it comes from: **Sequence changed for this set** or
**Sequence of the band**.

In live mode, setDING shows the whole song in its own order; the parts that do not
appear in the sequence are dimmed. If you would rather not see them, switch off
**Show parts outside the sequence** in the display settings: setDING then shows only
the parts of the sequence and names the hidden parts in the song's header. This also
applies when you start a single song with **Live**: then the sequence of the band
chosen on the Sets tab or in the band arrangement counts, otherwise the song's own. Key and tempo stay as the
song is written there.

## Another language version

If a song has [language versions](/en/songs/library/#language-versions), the set can
play another one tonight: **⋮ → Switch language version …** next to the song lists
the other versions with the key they would have in this set; a tap puts the chosen
version into the set. The set's transposition stays, and the band arrangement of the
new version applies. If that gives another key, setDING says so, e.g. *Switched to:
German, in E instead of D in this set*. A song sequence changed for this set stays
as long as the new version has all its parts; otherwise it is removed and setDING
says so.

## How did we play it last time?

A song in a set whose date has passed counts as **played**. On the day of the gig,
setDING keeps the key, tempo and sequence the band played it with. Later changes of
the band arrangement or the song do not change that anymore. It is kept when someone
of the band's leaders or set planners syncs, at the latest 14 days after the gig.

- **On the song:** tap **Played** at the top (the clock icon with the number). You
  see how often the band played the song, in total and in the last 12 months, and
  the latest gigs with date, set, key, tempo and sequence. A key other than the
  band's key today is highlighted. A "≈" before the key means the set has no kept
  values (e.g. an older set); then setDING shows how the band would play the song
  today.
- **At the key in a set:** the top of the key choice says in which key the band
  played the song last and most often.
- **When adding songs:** each song says when the band last played it, or "Never
  played".

If you change the key or the sequence in a past set afterwards, the correction
counts for the history too.

When several bands of the organization play the song, the **Played** panel shows
under **Other bands** which band played it last when, in which key and how often,
as of the last sync (without their sets). Admins and librarians of the organization
find the songs no band played under **Library usage** in the ⋮ menu of the song
list, and can move them to the trash.

### The band's repertoire

On **Sets**, the **Repertoire** icon at the top opens an overview from the band's
past sets:

- **Most played:** the songs the band played most often, in the last 12 months or
  all time.
- **Not played for a while:** songs the band played, but not for a long time. The
  band's leaders and set planners set when this applies with the stopwatch icon at the
  top (default: 6 months).
- **Never played:** songs of the library that were in no past set of the band.

Tap a song to open it. The song list knows the band's plays too: sort by **Last
played** or filter with **Played**, the last filter above the list, by **Never
played** or **Not played for a while**. **All** shows all songs again.

## PDF or chords in live mode

If a song has PDF attachments, an icon next to the key shows what you see in live
mode: the ChordPro text or one of the PDFs. Tap it to switch. This choice is yours
alone and applies in all sets.
